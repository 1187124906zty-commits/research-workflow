## What those originals actually change in the repair

Coleman's Methods opens with the choice of a continuum model and the excluded physical processes before introducing the effective source (p4). Its key detail is retained in the main text because the source formulation is the contribution; pp13–14 explain the isotherm search through which the new source responds to the solved field. The p13 bridge, beginning “By constraining the radius…”, identifies which inputs become fixed and which parameters remain available for calibration, then p15 explains why that changes calibration. Transfer that reasoning to this manuscript: explain why eta stays fixed and what the L/H changes isolate. Do **not** copy the long implementation subsection when the present paper does not claim an implementation advance. The property table at p20 puts parameter values immediately before comparisons.

Plotkowski's p7 opening selects pool width as a convenient initial verification metric and states its role. The p16 bridge begins “While the transient melt pool width is a convenient metric…” and explains why geometry alone is insufficient for solidification interpretation before introducing gradients and interface velocity. Its p20 grain-structure case then supplies a distinct physical comparison. This is the closest usable structural lesson for the present paper: first test geometry, then explain what rear thresholds and the x/v transformation add, while keeping those derived quantities distinct from a thermal measurement. The limitation passage on p25 identifies consequences of specific model assumptions, rather than disclaiming every earlier sentence.

Myers starts Methods from the measurement law (p2), then image processing, independent instrument standards and the LPBF conditions. In the simulation comparison, p7's “Once the two coefficients were fit…” links the geometry fit to an **additional** temperature-profile comparison; the detailed simulation setup/fitting and parameter summary are assigned to SI.1/Table S1, and ex-situ measurements to SI.6. The main text retains which parameters were fit and the geometry-versus-temperature contrast. This directly supports putting the present calibration/observation definition in the main text and package/version details in the appendix. Myers's p8 limitations starts from temperature uncertainty and camera sensitivity and develops the specific failure modes; it does not turn the whole Discussion into caveats.

Hooper's p4 “To calculate temperature…” leads from the measurement equation to calibration, followed by a separately named Validation subsection and then test geometry (p5). The Results unit on hatching starts from the physical scan location, not a panel inventory. Its p7 heading leads to spatial profiles, whereas p10's “In Fig. 14 temperature-time histories…” explicitly switches to fixed locations and a pulse-relative time origin. Preserve that semantic distinction here when moving from phase span to passage time. Hooper has seven Methods subsections: this is counterevidence to a universal small-subsection rule. Four subsections are chosen for this study because its implementation is supporting machinery, while its study design, closure and observers are the scientific content.

These are observed editorial practices and this task's chosen architecture.
