## Historical line and counterevidence that changes the argument

1. **Moving-source heat transfer and conservative phase change are established.** Van Elsen §3 separates constant-property analytical source studies from temperature-dependent finite-difference studies. Their Eq32–35 treatment conserves phase-change energy; §5 reports enthalpy as their most stable and fastest-converging implementation. The analytical point solution has a singular source temperature and serves as a far-field/reference comparison, not proof of accuracy near a finite Gaussian source. Their latent-heat importance results are for Ti-6Al-4V and low-conductivity powder conditions; they cannot be imported quantitatively to IN625.
2. **AM Bench conduction calibration is already a research line.** Kollmannsberger §4.2 obtains geometry agreement with source-shape degrees of freedom. Once measured beam input fixes those freedoms, isotropic predictions reach about 19.3% dimensional deviation. §4.3 then calibrates directional conductivity factors on B and compares A/C with fixed factors. Table10 reports maximum geometry deviation 6.49%; its conclusion explicitly says the model is not valid to predict the temperature distribution within the melt pool. Neither its absorptivity nor its directional factors can be copied into the present source/mesh/closure.
3. **The experiment defines different observables, not a joint observed 3D ellipsoid.** Lane §2.3 uses a second-derivative inflection to identify the rear freezing point; length extends to a front crossing of assumed 1290°C. §5 P70 recommends AMMT-20μs tracks for length and AMMT-100μs tracks for cross-sections. §4.3 P63–64 uses assumed longitudinal variation contributions because longitudinal depth/width variation was not measured directly. Expanded uncertainty uses k=2; do not replace it with a formal model-prediction distribution or independent repeated-track confidence interval.
4. **Effective source calibration can represent omitted physics.** Ross's verified abstract describes fitting a volumetric source that incorporates effective absorption and vapour-depression penetration, rather than resolving them from first principles. The present fitted scalar eta is therefore an effective model parameter conditional on source and closure. Ross supports this basic framing; detailed claims remain bounded by the currently available abstract.
5. **Geometry agreement does not identify thermal mechanism.** Myers §4.1 pp7–9 and Fig8 show multiple Fresnel/accommodation parameter combinations with good ex-situ geometric agreement but substantially different thermal profiles. Thermal data narrow the acceptable combinations. Their comparison also discusses plume obstruction and a constant-surface-tension model that neglects Marangoni flow. This is counterevidence to claiming parameter truth from geometry agreement, not evidence that a particular missing mechanism causes this case's residuals.

## Direct writing and figure transfer

- Introduction: state the modeling use and limitation early; establish the existing conduction/source/anisotropy line; identify the current question as closure-response and observable transfer. Do not say no prior work studied temperature-dependent properties or modeled the benchmark.
- Methods: give governing enthalpy, moving frame, phase interval, property continuations, Gaussian normalization, boundary conditions and measurement operators before implementation details. State the single B-length calibration and which data remain diagnostic. Compress software orchestration into reproducibility/supporting material.
- Results: show experimental comparisons and their operator definitions first, then case/velocity response, then closure-factorial evidence and thermal-history implications. Numerical verification is necessary support, not the headline contribution.
- Discussion: compare residual patterns with the calibrated prior model, explain closure response at the supported model-internal level, and name unresolved alternatives (source shape/measurement operator/property extrapolation/omitted flow). Avoid assigning discrepancies uniquely to Marangoni flow or evaporation.
- Figures: keep a visible distinction between thermographic length and metallographic width/depth; consistent scales and source-defined uncertainty; full captions with condition, model/data operator and comparison role. Myers Fig8 is a useful design model: organize several conditions under one question, share curve semantics and include a spatial panel linking profiles to geometry. Its many panels have different scientific jobs; do not reduce this case to one bar chart or add panels solely to match the count.
- Explanatory figure: a model/observation schematic should show material passage through the moving heat source, solidus/liquidus locations and which experimental operators are compared. It may explain inferred closure response; it must not show an unmeasured liquid-flow field as this study's result.
