## The shared scientific tension

Laser-processing thermal models are useful because temperature and solidification conditions connect controllable process settings to the structure produced. The same physical complexity that makes those conditions important makes affordable simulation difficult. Geometry is an accessible and informative constraint, and conduction-based models can reproduce it well under suitable source and material assumptions. A geometry comparison nevertheless answers a narrower question than the reliability of the inferred field, cooling path or solidification interpretation. In a calibrated model, material closure, effective energy deposition and the measurement operator all affect this inference.

This is an established tension, not a newly discovered failure of geometric calibration. Myers2023 already shows that geometry-fitting parameter combinations can yield different thermal profiles. Whalen2021 already modifies material properties and infers uncertain apparent input parameters. Hong2026 already diagnoses conditional conduction-model failure without geometric fitting. The paper should acknowledge these contributions before identifying its own specific experiment in model space.

## The bounded increment supported here

Keep the existing B length calibration fixed, retain the model's source/phase assumptions, and change the two high-temperature property continuations separately and jointly. Compare outputs that carry different diagnostic information:

1. Length fit versus width/depth shape agreement, then frozen-parameter A/C comparisons.
2. Conductivity continuation versus sensible-heat continuation and their finite-difference interaction.
3. Rear solidus and liquidus displacement together versus the separation between them.
4. The spatial phase interval versus material passage time obtained from known scan speed.

This makes the computed response interpretable within one explicitly defined nonlinear enthalpy case. It is a controlled decomposition, rather than a new heat equation, a new sensitivity field, a newly discovered geometry/profile ambiguity or a physically identified liquid-property function. The branches are not recalibrated to equal geometry. Their differences cannot be presented as an experiment proving several equally fitted branches with different thermal histories.

The key scientific reading of the result is that a large fitted-length response can mainly be a common displacement of the tail boundaries; it need not indicate a comparable enlargement of the axial phase interval. A change in scan speed can also give different material passage times even for similar spatial intervals. These distinctions answer the opening concern about thermal interpretation more directly than a general list of numerical sensitivities.

## Required comparisons in the argument

- Credit Myers's actual geometry/profile observation; explain that our perturbations are fixed-calibration branches rather than equally refitted parameters.
- Compare the diagnostic meaning of Yang's one-factor material sensitivity with our separate high-temperature functions and their nonlinear interaction. Do not call all existing work constant-property or geometry-only: Whalen and Coleman are direct counterexamples.
- Compare Hong's feasibility/failure criterion with our field-level tail decomposition. A stronger claim that current discrepancy establishes missing flow or keyholing would require absorption/source controls or independent thermal information.
- Use Soares and Lane to explain why an apparently strong correlation or a fitted dimension need not establish agreement of another observable. Do not combine incompatible uncertainty populations into a significance claim.
- Include the successful source-transfer route of Ross/Kusano/Coleman as a scientifically useful competing explanation. A future source-versus-closure comparison should hold the calibration target/operator and material inputs common before interpreting differences.
- Keep sensor-to-field mapping visible when discussing Weeks and Myers; a camera discrepancy need not isolate thermal physics.
