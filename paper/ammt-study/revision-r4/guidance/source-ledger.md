## MIT_ABSTRACT: Journal Article: Abstract

Original: https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/broad/commkit/journal-article-abstract/

Provenance: Broad Research Communication Lab on the MIT domain; page states adaptation from MIT Biological Engineering Communication Lab.

Actually read: Complete substantive guide: When to Write the Abstract, Purpose and Abstract Formula; linked annotated PDFs not read.

Local record: `sources/mit-abstract-fetch.txt`. Date: 2026-10-02.

- Locator: **When to Write the Abstract**; local text line 17.
- Short original quotation: “write your abstract last.”
- Supported principle: Distill the actual completed sections and subtract/consolidate findings until the crucial results remain.
- Agent use: Draft from the current Results/Discussion; rank possible quantitative anchors by what conclusion each establishes.
- Boundary: Writing last is a tactic, not an exclusive sequence; a working abstract can guide earlier work.

- Locator: **Abstract Formula > Implications**; local text line 35.
- Short original quotation: “Describe how your findings influence our understanding of the relevant field”
- Supported principle: Context, question, approach, selected findings and implications jointly make the abstract informative.
- Agent use: End on the concrete understanding or decision changed by the finding, including a useful negative finding.
- Boundary: The six-component formula and suggested sentence ranges are this teaching model; do not impose six sentences or a fixed number of numerical results.

## UNC_PARAGRAPHS: Paragraphs

Original: https://writingcenter.unc.edu/tips-and-tools/paragraphs/

Provenance: University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill Writing Center, College of Arts and Sciences.

Actually read: Complete substantive handout: definition, thesis/control, organization options, five-step illustration, all troubleshooting sections and reuse footer.

Local record: `sources/unc-paragraphs.txt`. Date: 2026-10-02.

- Locator: **What is a paragraph?**; local text line 17.
- Short original quotation: “Length and appearance do not determine whether a section in a paper is a paragraph.”
- Supported principle: Unity/coherence around a controlling idea, adequate evidence and explanation define a paragraph; completion can be a conclusion or transition.
- Agent use: Reverse-outline each paragraph by its inference and supporting evidence, then remove or relocate unrelated claims.
- Boundary: The five-step illustration is an example, not five sentences; the topic sentence can occur later; do not force every paragraph to end with a gap or summary.

## UNC_SCIENTIFIC_STYLE: Scientific Writing

Original: https://writingcenter.unc.edu/tips-and-tools/sciences/

Provenance: University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill Writing Center, College of Arts and Sciences.

Actually read: Complete substantive handout: precision, word choice, detail, quantification, clarity, sentence structure, verbosity, objectivity/voice and limitations; footer.

Local record: `sources/unc-sciences.txt`. Date: 2026-10-02.

- Locator: **Word and phrasing choice**; local text line 32.
- Short original quotation: “repetition is preferable to ambiguity.”
- Supported principle: Choose terms for their precise scientific meaning, keep subjects and actions legible, include necessary details and avoid unsupported generalization.
- Agent use: Check title noun relationships and collocations against their intended physical meaning; keep stable terms rather than decorative synonyms.
- Boundary: Its sentence/preposition rules are heuristics, not automatic thresholds. Objective claims depend on evidence and scope, not passive voice.

- Locator: **Passive voice**; local text line 75.
- Short original quotation: “Currently, the active voice is preferred in most scientific fields”
- Supported principle: Active voice can be clear and acceptable; editorial preferences vary. Passive can be useful but may become awkward.
- Agent use: Choose the grammatical subject to clarify the scientific action; never invent what an author did, and do not equate we with subjectivity.
- Boundary: This statement is writing-center advice, not a verified present-day survey of every journal; check the actual target requirement.

## PLOS_STRUCTURE: Ten simple rules for structuring papers

Original: https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1005619

Provenance: Brett Mensh and Konrad Kording, PLOS Computational Biology 13(9):e1005619 (2017); publisher original scholarly advice article; DOI10.1371/journal.pcbi.1005619.

Actually read: Overview, Introduction, full Rules1–10 and Discussion; relevant Figure1/Table1 captions in text; image pixels not inspected; linked correction not substantively read.

Local record: `sources/plos-structure.txt`. Date: 2026-10-02.

- Locator: **Rule 1**; local text line 39.
- Short original quotation: “make the claim and/or model as simple as the data and logic can support but no simpler.”
- Supported principle: A central contribution focuses title and whole paper; complexity should follow evidence, and one contribution can be multifaceted.
- Agent use: Write a contribution sentence and trace it to comparisons, figures and limits before revising prose.
- Boundary: Central contribution need not be improved accuracy or a new algorithm. This advice is not the target journal author guide.

- Locator: **Rule 7**; local text line 76.
- Short original quotation: “Every scientific argument has its own particular logical structure”
- Supported principle: Results form a sequence of evidence-supported statements, not a chronology of work; negative/hypothesis-disproving findings can advance the argument.
- Agent use: Select result units by what question they answer and connect them to the central contribution.
- Boundary: CCC and gap-ending paragraphs are advocated defaults, not universal paragraph templates. No fixed title/abstract numbers or paragraph counts follow.

Observed license: Article explicitly CC BY; unrestricted use/distribution/reproduction with author/source credit.

Reuse: Brief quotation and attributed paraphrase are used; an adapted scheme must be labeled our adaptation. Pedagogy references belong to guidance, not the study bibliography.
