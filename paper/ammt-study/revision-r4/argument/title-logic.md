# R4 title logic

The existing title can be retained grammatically: **High-temperature thermophysical properties and the thermal interpretation of melt-pool geometry in IN625**. No canonical title has been changed by this task.

The title is a noun phrase with two coordinated heads, "properties" and "interpretation". A scientific title need not contain a finite verb. Its grammar is complete as a nominal title; the editorial question is whether its breadth matches the paper's argument. Here, "thermal interpretation" accurately describes the contribution, while "high-temperature thermophysical properties" names the constitutive issue without claiming measured liquid properties.

The coordinator's preferred relational candidate is acceptable and is recommended over the coordinated-object options below:

**Thermophysical-property effects on melt-pool geometry and cooling histories in IN625 laser processing**

The grammatical head is **effects**. **Thermophysical-property** specifies the varied constitutive description; **on melt-pool geometry and cooling histories** identifies the outputs; **in IN625 laser processing** specifies the material and process. Unlike a title that coordinates only research objects, the preposition **on** expresses the studied relation. The title does not need a finite verb. "Effects" is earned within the controlled fixed-source comparison because the continuation choices are changed separately with the phase law and numerical configuration held fixed. The abstract supplies that model and continuation scope.

"Cooling histories" is earned as a modeled output: retained centerline profiles and their material-passage transformations describe cooling through the selected phase interval. It does not imply direct thermometry, independent history validation or fluid-parcel trajectories. This terminology is broader than the derived interval-mean rate alone, so it should remain only if the manuscript retains those profiles/history transformations. The supplied R4 manuscript and refreshed evidence retain them. No additional title optimization is necessary.

An idiomatic broader candidate that names the actual domain relation is:

**Thermophysical properties, melt-pool geometry and cooling histories in laser processing of IN625**

Its three coordinated noun heads are "properties", "geometry" and "histories". The final phrase places all three in one process and material; it does not create a missing predicate or an incomplete sentence. The title describes the relationship investigated in the paper without fitting the title to the four-corner design. The main tradeoff is that it names the three research objects but leaves their exact relationship to the abstract.

A second broader option puts the paper's intellectual action first:

**Interpreting melt-pool geometry and cooling histories in laser processing of IN625**

Here "Interpreting" is the head of a gerund phrase; "geometry and cooling histories" is its coordinated object, and "in laser processing of IN625" defines the domain. It conveys the earned interpretation contribution but leaves the constitutive variable implicit.

The disciplinary collocations have been checked against the actual sources: "thermophysical properties" occurs in Mills's article title and the supplier's property terminology; "melt pool" and "thermal history" occur in Van Elsen's Introduction; "melt pool temperature and cooling rates" is Hooper's article title; "laser material processing" is in Van Elsen's title. "Cooling histories" is consistent with the source's temperature--time histories, and the manuscript defines the present history by a translating-field mapping. The titles do not suggest direct thermometry.

A narrower candidate, only if the coordinator elects to specify the modeling focus, is:

**Thermal interpretation of IN625 melt-pool geometry under high-temperature property continuations**

This alternative makes the continuation choices explicit and gives the interpretation one grammatical head. It is not required to repair the current title, and no title change has been applied. Neither title promises improved prediction, a new solver or validated melt-pool temperatures.

MIT's recommendation to condense the main message supports the choice of a relational title. The specific "effects on" parsing is the project's evidence-based editorial judgment; the university guide does not certify this wording or require a full-sentence title. The recommended title is suitable for coordinator/reviewer assessment and need not be optimized further.
