 \section{Conclusions}
 
-High-temperature material-function choices change the melting extent and rear freezing interval of a length-calibrated IN625 conduction field on different scales. At the retained fitted source power, replacing linear conductivity extrapolation by a constant endpoint value lengthens the pool chiefly through downstream displacement of the rear solidus and liquidus together. Changing sensible specific heat partly offsets that extension, while all four property schemes remain wider and shallower than the metallographic means. The much smaller change in rear separation depends on the partner function. Matched axial refinement preserves the large boundary displacement but leaves the small conductivity-driven separation increase at constant-extrapolated specific heat unresolved in continuum direction. A substantial increase in pool length therefore need not represent a comparable widening of the adopted freezing interval.

Geometry comparisons assess the fusion shape; physical assessment of the rear crossings and their time mapping requires thermal observations with corresponding temperature intervals and trajectories. These distinct assessment targets clarify the thermal information that must be evaluated when a geometry-calibrated conduction field supplies inputs to subsequent solidification calculations.
