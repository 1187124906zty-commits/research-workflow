# Journal readers and evidence-shaped writing

Use original journal instructions and related published papers as evidence. Verify title, year, DOI/official URL and passages actually used. Publisher full text through an existing lawful session can use `sciencedirect-live-session-fetcher` when installed and applicable. Search a handful of representative papers with different methods or outcomes rather than choosing only a single admired template.

Record a compact journal profile: scope and reader questions; expected type of contribution; typical evidence/comparisons; section and figure logic; terminology/notation conventions; explicit submission requirements; sources and sample limits. Distinguish editorial requirements from inferred conventions and personal preferences. Reassess fit after results change.

The argument outline should connect each main claim with the evidence, assumptions, figure/table and limitation that permit it. A result gap is a question for a bounded evidence task, not a blank to be filled with plausible prose. Keep observed/calibrated/predicted/validated language distinct. Interpret contradictory results rather than removing them.

For a weak effect, decide whether the claim is descriptive, uncertain or unsupported based on uncertainty and checks; do not automatically demand heroic accuracy. For a strong claimed effect with a failed implementation or condition mismatch, more persuasive writing cannot establish it. A negative finding can become the contribution if the comparison and boundary are credible.

Review the draft from an informed adjacent reader's perspective: What question is answered? Why this method? What is compared? What supports the explanation? Where does it hold? How could I reproduce or challenge it? Also check sources and figure/data revisions, calibration/validation roles, and whether abstract/conclusion exceed the tested scope. Report submission readiness separately from scientific truth and from layout completion.
